# Biodiversity Indicators

## Pollinators and pesticide use
Insecticides cause both direct mortality and sublethal behavioral effects on pollinators, and broad-spectrum products also harm non-target predatory and parasitoid insects that would otherwise provide natural pest control. This creates a feedback loop: reduced natural pest control increases perceived pressure to apply more pesticide, which further suppresses pollinators and natural predators.
Source: IPBES (2016), Assessment Report on Pollinators, Pollination and Food Production, https://www.ipbes.net/assessment-reports/pollinators.

## Habitat connectivity
Connected natural habitat allows larger viable populations and enables species to move between patches in response to seasonal or climatic pressure. This landscape-ecology relationship holds across taxa, with well-documented effects on both general species richness and bird diversity specifically. Fragmentation from deforestation or land conversion is the primary driver of connectivity loss.
Source: IPBES (2019), Global Assessment Report on Biodiversity and Ecosystem Services, https://www.ipbes.net/global-assessment.

## Crop diversity and natural pest control
Diverse cropping systems provide varied structural and floral resources that support populations of natural pest predators and parasitoids, a mechanism known as conservation biological control. Monoculture systems lack this resource diversity and tend to rely more heavily on chemical pest control as a result.
Source: Landis, Wratten & Gurr (2000), "Habitat management to conserve natural enemies of arthropod pests in agriculture", Annual Review of Entomology 45:175-201, https://doi.org/10.1146/annurev.ento.45.1.175.

## Heat stress on pollinators
Temperature extremes increase pollinator mortality and disrupt normal foraging activity, compounding other stressors such as pesticide exposure and habitat loss. Crop yield is also directly affected when heat stress during flowering or grain-fill exceeds a crop's specific thermal threshold.
Source: IPBES (2016), Assessment Report on Pollinators, Pollination and Food Production, https://www.ipbes.net/assessment-reports/pollinators; IPCC AR6 Working Group II, Climate Change 2022: Impacts, Adaptation and Vulnerability, https://www.ipcc.ch/report/ar6/wg2/.

## Wetlands as biodiversity hotspots
Wetland ecosystems support species richness disproportionate to their land area, functioning as biodiversity hotspots and critical habitat for migratory species. Nutrient pollution from agricultural runoff is a major driver of wetland biodiversity decline through eutrophication.
Source: IPBES (2018), Assessment Report on Land Degradation and Restoration, https://www.ipbes.net/node/28328.
