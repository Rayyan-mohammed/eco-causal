# Human Impact

## Fertilizer, runoff, and water quality
Nitrogen and phosphorus applied beyond crop uptake capacity, or applied shortly before heavy rainfall, leaches or runs off into nearby waterways. This nutrient loading drives eutrophication, which reduces species richness in aquatic and wetland ecosystems downstream of farmland.
Source: FAO (2018), Water Pollution from Agriculture: A Global Review, https://www.fao.org/newsroom/detail/Pollutants-from-agriculture-a-serious-threat-to-world's-water/en; IPBES (2018), Assessment Report on Land Degradation and Restoration, https://www.ipbes.net/node/28328.

## Fertilizer and soil acidification
Sustained high-rate application of ammonium-based nitrogen fertilizer over multiple growing seasons acidifies soil. Below roughly pH 5.5, this acidification reduces earthworm populations and the soil-structuring benefits they provide.
Source: FAO Global Soil Partnership, Voluntary Guidelines for Sustainable Soil Management (acidifying fertilizer management), https://www.fao.org/global-soil-partnership/en (a single dedicated 'soil acidification' guidance note was not independently located in this pass).

## Irrigation and salinization
In poorly drained soils or arid and semi-arid climates with high evaporation, irrigation water leaves behind dissolved salts as it evaporates, gradually raising soil salinity. This impairs both crop yield, through osmotic stress on roots, and soil microbial diversity.
Source: FAO Global Soil Partnership, International Network on Salt-Affected Soils (INSAS), https://www.fao.org/global-soil-partnership/insas/en.

## Land degradation as a composite outcome
Land degradation aggregates the combined loss of soil fertility, structure, and water-holding capacity from multiple pressures, including overgrazing, deforestation, and unsustainable cultivation. Degraded land supports lower crop yield and lower species richness simultaneously, since both depend on the same underlying productive capacity.
Source: IPBES (2018), Assessment Report on Land Degradation and Restoration, https://www.ipbes.net/node/28328.

## Pesticide use and non-target effects
Pesticide application intended to control crop pests also suppresses pollinators and natural pest predators through direct toxicity and sublethal effects. This can create a reinforcing cycle where reduced natural pest control increases reliance on further pesticide application.
Source: IPBES (2016), Assessment Report on Pollinators, Pollination and Food Production, https://www.ipbes.net/assessment-reports/pollinators.

## Multi-step reasoning risk in agricultural advisory
Advisory systems that retrieve individually true facts can still assemble them into an invalid recommendation if the interaction between facts is not itself checked. A documented example is recommending water-intensive interventions such as dense cover cropping in a semi-arid, low-rainfall context: soil organic carbon and moisture retention are both individually well-supported outcomes of relevant interventions, but the specific combination breaks down under a rainfall condition that a fact-level check alone would not catch.
Source: FAO Global Soil Partnership, https://www.fao.org/global-soil-partnership/en (dryland cover cropping guidance), cross-referenced with IPCC AR6 Working Group II, https://www.ipcc.ch/report/ar6/wg2/ (water-limited system chapters).
