# Climate Factors

## Drought and soil moisture
Increasing drought frequency reduces the baseline water available to replenish soil moisture between rainfall events. Without supplemental irrigation or drought-adapted crop varieties, water stress during critical growth stages directly reduces yield.
Source: IPCC AR6 Working Group II, food security and land chapters.

## Rainfall and vegetation cover
Higher rainfall generally supports greater plant biomass and ground cover, up to a site-specific saturation point beyond which waterlogging becomes the limiting factor instead. This makes rainfall level a key determinant of which land management interventions are appropriate for a given site.
Source: FAO Land and Water assessments, general agroecology synthesis.

## Deforestation and regional rainfall
At landscape scale, sustained forest loss reduces evapotranspiration and weakens the moisture-recycling process that sustains regional rainfall patterns. This is a slow-acting, regional-scale effect that develops over years to decades, distinct from the immediate, local effects of deforestation on erosion and habitat loss.
Source: IPCC AR6 Working Group I, land-atmosphere feedback chapters.

## Temperature extremes and crop physiology
Crops have species-specific thermal thresholds beyond which flowering success and grain fill are impaired. As the frequency of temperature extremes rises, yield losses become more likely during the crop's most heat-sensitive growth stages, independent of water availability.
Source: IPCC AR6 Working Group II.

## Semi-arid rainfall thresholds and intervention choice
Climate classification places semi-arid systems roughly in the 300 to 600mm annual rainfall range. Below approximately 400mm, water-competing interventions such as dense cover cropping carry meaningful tradeoff risk against crop water availability, while moisture-conserving interventions such as agroforestry tend to perform more reliably.
Source: FAO Global Soil Partnership guidance on cover cropping in dryland systems; FAO (2017), Agroforestry for Landscape Restoration.
