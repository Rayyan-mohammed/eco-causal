# Land Use and Land Cover

## Monoculture and crop diversity
Land under intensive monoculture, by definition, carries lower crop diversity than rotated or intercropped systems. Reduced crop diversity in turn removes the varied flowering periods and structural habitat that support pollinators and natural pest predators, and diversified rotations are associated with lower rates of land degradation because they reduce pest and disease buildup and maintain more consistent ground cover.
Source: FAO (2019), The State of the World's Biodiversity for Food and Agriculture, https://openknowledge.fao.org/items/b355c300-72ed-4a63-be07-8295c80ec7f1.

## Agroforestry in low-rainfall systems
Integrating trees and shrubs into cropland, known as agroforestry, reduces evaporative water loss through canopy shading and increases infiltration through leaf litter and root channels. This makes it a particularly effective moisture-retention strategy in semi-arid to sub-humid climates, where water-competing interventions like dense cover cropping carry more risk. Agroforestry also adds flowering resources and structural habitat complexity that support pollinator populations.
Source: FAO (2017), Agroforestry for Landscape Restoration, https://doi.org/10.4060/i7374e; Klein et al. (2007), Proceedings of the Royal Society B 274(1608):303-313, https://doi.org/10.1098/rspb.2006.3721.

## Tillage and erosion
Mechanical tillage disturbs soil structure and leaves bare soil exposed between planting and canopy closure, increasing vulnerability to both wind and water erosion. This effect compounds with organic carbon loss from the disturbance itself.
Source: FAO (2019), Soil Erosion: The Greatest Challenge for Sustainable Soil Management, https://openknowledge.fao.org/items/6c070e1e-6533-4b7e-ba5f-a2f21a0e59ff.

## Deforestation and habitat fragmentation
Clearing forest for agriculture or settlement fragments the remaining habitat into smaller, more isolated patches. Reduced habitat connectivity limits species movement and the viable population sizes an area can support, lowering both general species richness and bird diversity specifically. At landscape scale and over years to decades, sustained deforestation also reduces regional rainfall by weakening forest evapotranspiration and moisture recycling; this is a slow, regional effect rather than an immediate local one.
Source: IPBES (2019), Global Assessment Report on Biodiversity and Ecosystem Services, https://www.ipbes.net/global-assessment; IPCC AR6 Working Group I, Climate Change 2021: The Physical Science Basis, https://www.ipcc.ch/report/ar6/wg1/ (land-atmosphere feedback chapters).

## Overgrazing
Livestock grazing pressure that exceeds the land's carrying capacity compacts soil, reducing porosity and aggregate stability, and removes vegetation faster than it can regenerate. The resulting bare ground is then more exposed to erosion, compounding the initial degradation.
Source: FAO, Sustainable rangeland management in Sub-Saharan Africa: Guidelines to Good Practice, https://www.fao.org/family-farming/detail/en/c/1632938/ (exact publication year not independently verified in this pass).

## Wetlands
Wetlands support disproportionately high species richness relative to their land area and also filter nutrients from surrounding runoff before it reaches open water, reducing downstream pollution loads. Loss of wetland extent removes both of these functions simultaneously.
Source: IPBES (2018), Assessment Report on Land Degradation and Restoration, https://www.ipbes.net/node/28328; FAO (2018), Water Pollution from Agriculture: A Global Review, https://www.fao.org/newsroom/detail/Pollutants-from-agriculture-a-serious-threat-to-world's-water/en.

## Deforestation's direct effects on species richness and land degradation
Beyond the fragmentation/connectivity pathway, clearing forest directly shrinks the total habitat area available, and area loss alone reduces the number of species a landscape can support — a separate, well-established mechanism from connectivity loss. The same loss of root systems and organic litter inputs that stabilized soil also directly contributes to broader land degradation, not just localized erosion.
Source: IPBES (2019), Global Assessment Report on Biodiversity and Ecosystem Services, https://www.ipbes.net/global-assessment; IPBES (2018), Assessment Report on Land Degradation and Restoration, https://www.ipbes.net/node/28328.

## Irrigation as a direct yield lever in water-limited systems
Where rainfall alone is insufficient, supplemental irrigation directly relieves crop water stress. Globally, irrigated cropland produces yields averaging 76 percent higher than rainfed cropland, making it one of the most direct interventions available in a rainfed or water-limited baseline — though it must be weighed against the salinization and drainage risks documented elsewhere in this knowledge base.
Source: FAO, Land and Water Division, Irrigation overview, https://www.fao.org/land-water/water/agricultural-water-management/irrigation/en.
